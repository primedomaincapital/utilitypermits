Where utility permits actually get issued.

Last checked: November 18, 2026

Utility permitting sits at the crossing of three things: the queue of projects waiting to connect, the railroads and utilities that issue the permits, and the expediters who move the files. The links below cover all three — the queue research that sizes the wait, the fee schedules that price the crossing, the expediting firms that sell the speed, and the investment data that shows how much work is coming.

The queue: interconnection research and reform

Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory — Queued Up 2026: Characteristics of Power Plants Seeking Transmission Interconnection

The definitive annual count of the U.S. interconnection queue: 2,061 gigawatts across about 8,200 projects at the end of 2025, the mix by technology, and the outcome data showing only 13 percent of the 2000–2020 cohort ever reached operation.

Burns & McDonnell — How FERC Order 2023 Impacts the Generation Interconnection Process

A plain-English guide to the 2023 interconnection reform: first-ready, first-served cluster studies, the 150-day cluster study, enforceable deadlines, and the readiness requirements that now cost speculative projects real money.

Troutman Pepper Locke — Summary of FERC Order No. 2023 on Generator Interconnection Reform

The law firm's clause-by-clause summary of the July 28, 2023 rule: cluster study mechanics, cost allocation by proportional impact, the 20 percent network-upgrade deposit, and the compliance filing timeline.

Government Computer News — Battery storage queue backlog remains above 2,000 gigawatts despite FERC reforms

Reporting on the Queued Up 2026 data: the queue fell to 2,061 gigawatts by the end of 2025, PJM timelines stretched to roughly eight years, and battery storage still dominates the backlog.

The toll: railroad utility-permit fee schedules

CSX — Utility Permits

The railroad's dedicated utility-permits page: a $7,500 expedited review fee, on top of engineering and license fees, for an agreement within 15 working days.

Genesee & Wyoming — Utility Occupancies

The short-line operator's published schedule: six to eight weeks standard, one to two weeks expedited for $3,000 more, with a $1,000 application fee, a $2,250 engineering review fee, and a $1,750 right-of-entry fee.

Norfolk Southern / RailPros — Pipe & Wire Program FAQ

The pipe-and-wire fee list: $7,000 for an aerial wireline crossing, $8,000 underground, $9,000 for a pipeline crossing, with $3,000 supplements and a $500 charge for reviews after the first two.

The trade: permit-expediting firms and prices

CostOwl — The Cost and Benefits of Using a Building Permit Service

The baseline price guide for the trade: $200 to $1,000 for a typical expediting job, $200 to $400 for minor residential work, and $800 to $5,000 and up for commercial projects.

Nationwide Permit — The Ultimate Guide to Commercial Permit Expediting

A national firm's guide to commercial expediting: $500 to $5,000 per permit, $75 to $150 an hour, more than $15,000 for large projects, and $2,000 to $8,000 on the West Coast.

ClearPath Permits — New Jersey Permit Expediting

A New Jersey expediter built around utility work: PSE&G and Jersey Central Power & Light service-connection applications from $349 per permit, across all 21 counties, with 24-hour submission.

Best Permit Runners — Flat-Fee Permit Expediting Pricing

A Florida runner's published price list: electrical and plumbing permits at $300 each, small building permits at $450, large ones from $1,200, and inspections through close-out at $200.

PermitExpertsNYC — Permit Expediter Cost NYC, 2026 Pricing Guide

New York City pricing: $500 to $5,000 and up by permit type, with Department of Transportation permits at $500 to $2,000 and city filing fees of $280 to $2,100 on top.

TaskHatch — Start a Permit Project

A managed-filing service at the low end of the market: permit-only filing at $150, filing plus inspection coordination at $250, with resubmittals at $75.

The build: utility investment, solar, and housing permits

Edison Electric Institute — EEI Releases 2024 Financial Review

The trade group's annual review: member companies invested a record $178.2 billion in 2024, the 13th straight record year, and are projected to invest more than $1.1 trillion between 2025 and 2029.

Edison Electric Institute — Electric Companies to Invest Nearly $208B in 2025

The 2025 spending projection: nearly $208 billion in one year, with 91 gigawatts under construction and 488 gigawatts planned or proposed.

Solar Energy Industries Association / Wood Mackenzie — Solar Adds More New Capacity to the Grid in 2024 Than Any Energy Technology in Two Decades

The 2024 year-in-review: a record 50 gigawatts of new U.S. solar capacity, 66 percent of all new generating capacity added, with Texas leading at 11.6 gigawatts.

U.S. Census Bureau — 2025 Annual Building Permits

The official count: 1,431,616 new privately owned housing units authorized by building permits in 2025, with a total valuation of $378.99 billion.
